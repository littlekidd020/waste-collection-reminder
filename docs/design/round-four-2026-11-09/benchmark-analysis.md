# Benchmark study: six ADA 2026 iPhone apps, for a NZ bin-day app

Source: the first six iPhone App Store screenshots of each app, fetched through the iTunes lookup API on 9 Nov 2026 (36 images, each one viewed). All six apps were found. "The Outsiders: Athlete Tracker" is listed in the US store as "The Outsiders Fitness Tracker" (Gentler Stories d.o.o.); it is the same app. Caveat: these are marketing frames. Device mockups, headlines and award laurels belong to the store page, not the app. I judged only the UI inside the device frames, plus the full-bleed captures for grug and Camera.

---

## 1. Tide Guide: Charts & Tables (Condor Digital)

**Typography.** SF Pro Rounded-feeling sans, bold and semibold only. The hero reading "3.8′" is about 12% of the screen width, with "Rising Tide" below it in semibold and "High in 1 hr, 56 min" in secondary grey. Card eyebrows are small all-caps labels with an icon ("↑ RISING TIDE", "AIR TEMP", "WIND SPEED"). Times are compact ("5:13p", "10:51a"). About five type sizes are visible.
**Chrome.** A Liquid Glass floating tab-bar pill (Today / Charts / Tables / Solunar) where the selected tab sits in a lighter glass lens. The top row is three glass controls: a circular menu button, a location capsule "Chatham, Stage Harbor ⌄", and a circular ⋯ button. There is no large title, because the location is the title. Chrome takes roughly 20% of the screen.
**Layout.** On Today there are no cards: the numbers float centred and the tide curve runs full-bleed off both edges at the bottom. Charts and Tables stack 3 cards with about 16pt margins.
**Material.** A deep navy-to-teal vertical gradient. Cards are translucent with a 1px light stroke. Colour is carried by data: cyan curve, amber temperature fill, red "now" dot.
**Signature.** The sine-wave tide curve with a red "you are here" dot. The same curve appears in the app, widgets, Live Activity and Watch.
**Avoid copying.** The scrolling chip rows ("24H 2D 3D 5D 10D", "10 Days Sep Oct Nov") are dense. Small sunrise/moonrise icons sitting in tinted squares read as 2020-era weather apps.

## 2. Moonlitt: Moon Phase Tracker (Flipping Hues)

**Typography.** Neutral grotesk (SF-like), used sparingly. The main view has almost no text, only white capsule labels "Today" and "09:41 PM". The calendar uses semibold day numerals at about 4% of the width, with zodiac glyphs as tiny secondary marks. The eclipse sheet pairs label over value ("Duration" / "1h 39m 56s"), value in semibold. Three or four sizes per screen.
**Chrome.** Almost none. Circular glass buttons sit in the corners (compass "N", telescope, layers, ⋯). The tab control is a tiny centred floating glass pill with three icons only. Sheets are system Liquid Glass with a grabber and a segmented "Info | Times". Content is over 90% of the screen.
**Layout.** The hero is a photoreal 3D moon on a dotted arc above a glowing ground ellipse, placed slightly below centre in empty indigo space. The calendar is a 7-column grid of moon renders separated by hairline rules, with no cards.
**Material.** A deep indigo gradient, real moon textures, a soft-shaded 3D direction marker, and glowing orbit lines. Colour is monochrome indigo. The one accent is a copper "blood moon" in the grid.
**Signature.** Time as a draggable 3D sky scene: the object is the interface.
**Avoid copying.** The grey nested rounded rects in the eclipse sheet are the most generic part.

## 3. grug (Ocho)

**Typography.** A hand-drawn, wobbly lowercase marker font for everything. Body text is about 5.5% of the screen width per line with generous leading. The wordmark is "grug." with a full stop, and the signature/date line is "grug. 5.11". There are no eyebrows, no caps and no bold: one or two sizes in total. Numerals are hand-drawn too ("5.9", "5.10").
**Chrome.** Custom and hand-drawn. "grug." sits top left and a scribbled dot top right. The bottom row holds doodle icons for past days (sprout, cave, fire) with dates under them, and a hand-drawn oval share button with "↑ ☺" in the centre. Even the status-bar battery is redrawn as "(100)". There is no system tab bar or navigation bar.
**Layout.** One drawing in the upper third, one paragraph left-aligned at a roughly 13% left margin, and a large field of empty space. Zero cards.
**Material.** Flat. A different solid background each day (forest green, sky blue, mint, near-black, white), with the line colour changed to match (cream, navy, maroon, orange). No gradients and no shadows.
**Signature.** The daily palette swap plus line drawing: each day feels like a new page.
**Avoid copying.** Nothing reads as dated. The risk is that the naïve style needs real conviction; half-done, it looks unfinished.

## 4. The Outsiders (Gentler Stories)

**Typography.** SF Pro with heavy weights. "Today 25 January" puts the title in bold and the date in grey at the same size. The readiness band "95%" uses a large bold numeral with a small "%". Metrics use a monospaced numeral face ("0.84", "1.27", "22:34", "07:09"), which is the distinctive move. Small all-caps status labels have a green check ("✓ OPTIMAL", "NORMAL", "AS USUAL"). There is also a "Progress" large title. Five or six sizes.
**Chrome.** A floating dark glass tab pill (Today / Progress / Workouts) with the selected item highlighted in pink. Detail screens use circular glass back, calendar and filter buttons. There is no navigation bar background.
**Layout.** The top 35–40% of each screen is a full-bleed colour wash (magenta for readiness/sleep, green for load, blue for progress) that fades to black, with the verdict word ("Good", "Prime Readiness") and plain-language prose sitting on it. Below that are black sections with 2–5 dark rounded cards, and a dot-matrix week grid.
**Material.** Saturated top-glow gradients over true black, glowing chart endpoints, and a frosted 3D app icon.
**Signature.** Colour equals meaning: each metric gets its own hue, plus a one-word verdict and a sentence.
**Avoid copying.** The neon magenta-on-purple marketing look, and the row of five tiny metric tiles.

## 5. Structured (unorderly)

**Typography.** SF Pro. The header "13. May" is bold black at about 9% of the width, with "2026 ›" in coral. Task titles are semibold ("Wake Up", "Yoga") with grey time ranges above them ("7:00–7:15 AM (15 min)"). Weekday labels are small grey ("Mon Tue Wed"). Four sizes.
**Chrome.** A light floating tab pill (Inbox / Timeline / AI / Settings) using filled glyphs, beside a separate coral circular "+" FAB. The week strip is pinned in the header. Today is a coral filled circle around "13".
**Layout.** A vertical timeline. Each event is a coloured capsule whose height equals its duration, with an icon inside, threaded on a thin connector line. Text sits to the right and a hollow completion ring sits at the far right edge. All-day items are round grey bubbles in a horizontal row. The week view puts 7 timeline columns side by side, and future days are greyed.
**Material.** Flat white and light grey with blush-pink brand colour. Mostly no shadows, except a soft lift on widget and notification cards. One grainy rendered cloud illustration.
**Signature.** Duration-proportional colour capsules on a vertical thread.
**Avoid copying.** The coral FAB next to the tab pill, the Mon–Sun chip selector, and the "Once / Daily / Weekly / Monthly" segmented pill all look like 2021 SwiftUI defaults.

## 6. (Not Boring) Camera (Not Boring Software)

**Typography.** A geometric grotesk (Graphik/Neue Haas-like) for headlines. In the UI the voice is a monospaced, tracked all-caps label ("MACRO", "EXPOSURE", "SHUTTER SPEED", "ZEBRA STRIPES") paired with tiny geometric colour glyphs. Distances are short mono strings (".02m", "∞"). The picker labels "Top / Lens / Buttons" are large bold words with inactive items greyed. Two or three sizes.
**Chrome.** None of it is iOS chrome. Every control is a rendered 3D physical part: a knurled scroll wheel, a chunky yellow flash toggle with a "ϟ" glyph, round inset buttons ("A", sliders), and a toothed orange focus ring around the viewfinder. There is no tab bar.
**Layout.** The viewfinder is the hero, sitting in a dark machined body. Controls are recessed into the chassis rather than placed in cards.
**Material.** Claymation-like 3D renders with soft ambient occlusion, matte plastic, and colour swatches rendered as glossy spheres. A charcoal base with a single loud accent (orange, yellow or violet).
**Signature.** Software as a toy object: tactile, customisable hardware.
**Avoid copying.** It is fully skeuomorphic. Copied in part (one 3D button in a flat UI), it looks like 2012.

## Cross-app synthesis

These apps look current because each one picks a single, ownable visual object (a tide curve, a moon, a drawing, a colour wash, a capsule thread, a dial) and lets it carry the screen. Chrome shrinks to floating glass pills and circular corner buttons. None of them uses a classic navigation bar with a centred title and back chevron, and none uses a full-width opaque tab bar. Typography is quiet sans with one oversized numeral or verdict per screen. Labels are either small caps eyebrows (Tide Guide, Outsiders, Camera) or plain grey secondary text. Colour carries meaning (data colour, per-metric hue, daily palette) instead of decoration. Cards are used sparingly, and the best hero screens (Tide Guide Today, Moonlitt, grug) use none. For a bin-day app the obvious ownable object is the bin lid colour (red, yellow, green, glass blue), arranged as a fortnight rhythm. Render it with tone and tactility, not raw chroma.

### 10 craft rules for the bin-day app

1. **One hero per screen, no card around it.** On Today, the answer ("Tonight: Recycling + Rubbish") is the hero: big, centred or left-anchored, floating on the background like Tide Guide's "3.8′".
2. **Use one big number or word.** Size the hero at 10–15% of the screen width ("Tue", "Tonight", "2 days") and keep everything else at 3–4 smaller sizes, with a maximum of five per screen.
3. **Write a verdict plus one human sentence** (Outsiders). For example, "Out tonight" with "Collection is before 7am tomorrow; yellow lid only this week."
4. **Let colour mean something.** Bin colours are the only accents. Make the hero wash or background tint follow which bins are due this week, the way Outsiders and grug change palette per state.
5. **Keep chrome to floating Liquid Glass.** Use a 3-item floating tab pill (Today / Schedule / Guide), circular glass corner buttons, and the location or suburb as a capsule title like Tide Guide's "Chatham, Stage Harbor ⌄". Do not use a large title as well.
6. **Build one signature visual** and reuse it everywhere: app, widget, Live Activity, Watch. Tide Guide's curve is the model. For bins, try a fortnight rhythm strip of lid-coloured marks, or rendered lids.
7. **Make the Schedule a grid without boxes.** Use a 7-column grid with hairline week rules and rendered objects in the cells (Moonlitt's moon grid), with today marked by a single filled circle on the numeral. Do not give every day its own card.
8. **Use small caps eyebrows with a glyph** ("↺ THIS FORTNIGHT") at about 11–12pt, semibold, lightly tracked and secondary in colour. Use monospaced or tabular numerals for times and dates (Outsiders, Camera).
9. **Give depth through material, not stacked borders.** Use a deep, low-chroma gradient background or a full-bleed tint, translucent surfaces with a 1px light stroke, and soft rendered objects. Use at most one level of container.
10. **Be generous with emptiness.** grug and Moonlitt leave 40–50% of the screen empty, with 16–20pt margins and an asymmetric left-anchored text block. Empty space reads as confident.

### 8 things that make a screen look old

1. Settings-style list rows with chevrons and leading bell, bin or calendar icons in tinted rounded squares.
2. An opaque full-width tab bar with outline SF Symbols and labels, iOS 13 style. Any chrome with a hard top hairline.
3. Cards nested inside cards, especially grey rounded rects inside a sheet inside a card (Moonlitt's weakest screen).
4. Horizontally scrolling rows of pill chips for dates or ranges ("24H 2D 3D", "Mon Tue Wed"), and pill-shaped date buttons in general.
5. A coral circular "+" FAB floating beside the tab bar, or other Material-era add buttons.
6. Heavy editorial serif headlines in a blog typeface, or several display faces on one screen.
7. Full-chroma flat primaries (pure #FF0000 red, #FFFF00 yellow lids) on white, with drop shadows under every tile.
8. Half-skeuomorphism: one glossy 3D toggle or bin in an otherwise flat list UI. Commit to one rendering language (Camera) or none.
