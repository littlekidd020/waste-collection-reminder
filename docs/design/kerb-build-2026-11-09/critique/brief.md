You are an independent design critic reviewing screenshots of a shipping iPhone app, "Bin Day NZ", which tells New Zealand households which council kerbside bins go out and when. You have not seen the design brief and you must not be kind. The attached images are simulator captures of the built app (iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26): Today in light and dark, Today after the bins were swiped to the kerb, Schedule, Guide (light and dark).

Answer these questions in order, in plain prose, at most 600 words in total:

1. Date it. If you saw these screens with no context, what year would you say they were designed in, and which three visual cues drive that judgement most?
2. Craft. Name the five most visible craft faults, in order of how much they hurt: alignment, spacing rhythm, type hierarchy, colour, material (glass, shadows, gradients), rendering quality of the bins, legibility, consistency between screens. Point at exact places (screen, region, element) and say what a top studio would change.
3. Clarity. On Today, can a first-time user tell within two seconds whether to put bins out tonight, and which ones? What slows them down?
4. The bins. Do the rendered bins read as one physical set standing on one floor, or as stickers pasted on? Does the dark appearance hold up?
5. The strongest single moment in the set, and the weakest.
6. Three changes you would make before shipping, each in one sentence, each specific enough to act on.

Do not praise before criticising. Do not suggest adding more features. Do not comment on the status bar.
