# Bin Day NZ — six-week pilot and maintenance record

Prepared 6 September 2026. **No recruitment, household observations, partnerships or pilot results are recorded here.** This is a bounded protocol and fill-in record. Begin after critical date/calendar/reminder safety defects are resolved. Contacting participants is a separate authorised action.

## Study setup

Recruit 8–12 consenting Auckland households responsible for ordinary council kerbside bins. Include more than one weekday and fortnightly cycle, a recent move or address correction, and at least two people who benefit from large text or assistive technology. Invite fluent language reviewers for any advertised language being evaluated. These parameters support qualitative learning, not population estimates.

Use random household identifiers in the record. Keep recruitment contact details separate with restricted access and delete them after the agreed follow-up. Do not put home addresses, device coordinates, personal calendars or identifying screenshots in GitHub reports. Explain optional participation, withdrawal and what observations will be retained before recording anything.

## Weekly protocol

| Week | Activity | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Observe setup using the app and a Council-lookup/recurring-calendar comparison task | Completion without help, time, wrong matches, uncertainty, preference and reasons |
| 2 | Observe normal weekly and fortnightly use | Correct date, useful reminder/widget, any missed bin and whether the app caused it |
| 3 | Ask participants to perform a schedule edit or explain a hypothetical moved collection | Whether they find the action and understand which dates change; do not alter their real schedule for a test |
| 4 | Check reminder permission/recovery understanding and continued preference | Any silent expiration, confusion, need to reopen, or abandoned channel |
| 5 | Repeat source/date check; investigate outstanding problems | Time to diagnose, source evidence, correction and participant confirmation |
| 6 | Final short interview and outcome review | Continued choice, comparison with prior habit/calendar, remaining failures and maintenance cost |

Use a test profile for simulated disruptions and permission tasks. If no holiday occurs during the study, record that holiday delivery was not observed; complete deterministic source/exception tests separately. Do not make people risk an actual missed collection for research.

Report counts with denominators, distinguish observed events from self-reports and keep any inconclusive result. A delivered notification is not proof the person saw it or the council emptied the bin. App opens and downloads are secondary for a utility designed to require little attention. Use any App Store usage data with its opt-in/sample limitations.

## Maintenance record

Review support once each working week and before a relevant holiday. Check public sample properties and official exception notices before releases/holidays and when someone reports a source mismatch. Do not create an automated scraper or promise a response SLA until maintainable source access and capacity are established.

Prioritise calendar data loss and systemic wrong dates immediately when discovered; stop promotion and triage. For other issues, acknowledge through the existing support route, record the expected next review, and close only after the correction is checked. If the routine ceiling is exceeded for two consecutive weeks or the incident reserve is exhausted, pause expansion and revisit scope/funding; do not conceal unpaid emergency work.

Track actual cash expenses and hours separately. `Study cost = cash expenses + logged hours × chosen internal hourly cost`. Record the internal rate explicitly if one is used. Do not extrapolate revenue from installs, reviews or hypothetical donations.

Keep the core free while evidence is gathered. Only investigate one additional capability when observations support it. A second council needs maintainable authorised data access, verified samples/content and a capacity owner. A funded pilot needs a named buyer and written scope; none is assumed here.
